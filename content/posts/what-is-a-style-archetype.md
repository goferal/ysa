Most style advice answers the wrong question. It tells you what is in, what is flattering "for your body type" in the magazine sense, or what a capsule wardrobe should contain. None of that is about you.

A style archetype is the opposite. It is a description of *you*, built from three things that do not change with the season: your lines, your coloring, and your mood.

## Your lines

This is the Kibbe part. David Kibbe's system sorts bodies by how much they lean toward *yang* (long, sharp, angular) or *yin* (soft, rounded, small), and how those two blend. It is not about size. A tall, narrow woman and a petite, curvy one are simply different shapes, and the fabrics and silhouettes that fall into place on one will fight the other.

Once you know your type, a lot of mystery disappears. The blazer that never sat right. The dress everyone loved on your friend. It was never a personal failing. It was geometry.

## Your coloring

Color seasons describe the undertone, depth, and clarity of your skin, eyes, and hair, and then find the colors that agree with them. When the colors near your face agree with you, you look rested and awake. When they don't, you look tired, and so does the outfit.

This is the one people are most skeptical about, and the one they thank us for most.

## Your mood

Style essences are the personality layer. Two Soft Naturals in the same palette can dress completely differently, because one carries a romantic mood and the other a dramatic one. Essences give you the vocabulary for that: the details, textures, and accents that feel like you rather than a costume.

## Why all three

Any one of these lenses helps. Together they do something more useful: they give you a short, specific description of what suits you, in words you can take shopping. That description is your style archetype.
